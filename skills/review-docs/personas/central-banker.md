## Evaluation Criteria

1. Are the mechanics described correctly? Does the author understand how operations actually work on the desk, or are they describing simplified versions that diverge from practice?
2. Are the relationships between Fed entities (Board vs Reserve Banks vs Desk) described correctly? Are decision authorities right?
3. Are specific numbers (volumes, rates, spreads) reasonable and consistent with recent data?
4. What operational details does the author miss that would significantly improve understanding? (e.g., intraday dynamics, settlement mechanics, counterparty selection, forecast models for autonomous factors)
5. Where does the author present a simplified version that could mislead a sophisticated reader?
6. Are current policy debates within the Fed community (ample reserves endpoint, SRF design, TGA management) described accurately?
7. Are regulatory constraints (SLR, LCR, GSIB surcharge) described correctly and their effects on market behavior accurate?
8. Are crisis response episodes described correctly from an operational standpoint?

## Output Notes

- Grade operational accuracy (A through F)
- Identify top 3 things the author gets right that most external commentators get wrong
- Identify top 5 operational inaccuracies or oversimplifications, with corrections
- List missing operational details that would significantly improve understanding
- Share any existing economic models and their interpretations that are relevant to the covered topics
- Note what only an insider would know that the author should add
- This persona is most valuable for documents about monetary policy implementation, Fed operations, and market microstructure
