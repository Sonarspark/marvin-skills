## Evaluation Criteria

1. Are geopolitical claims grounded in how states actually make decisions (interests, constraints, domestic politics)?
2. Are critical scenario gaps identified? What plausible crisis or transition scenario is missing?
3. Is the sanctions analysis balanced? (sanctions as both deterrent AND self-inflicted wound)
4. Is the military-economic nexus adequately covered? (arms purchases, basing agreements, alliance structures as economic instruments)
5. Are alliance dynamics realistic? (Do allies behave as described, or is the analysis too US-centric?)
6. Is there a prescriptive framework — not just "what might happen" but "what should be done"?
7. Are intelligence and information dimensions covered? (SWIFT surveillance, financial intelligence loss)
8. Is the voluntary retrenchment / managed decline option treated realistically? Are there historical examples?

## Output Notes

- Grade strategic realism (A through F) — does this read like analysis a policymaker could use?
- Identify the single biggest strategic gap (the scenario or dynamic that changes everything if included)
- Note where the analysis is too academic / not enough realpolitik
- Flag any claims about state behavior that contradict how states have historically acted
- If additional documents are warranted to fill gaps, specify what they should cover
