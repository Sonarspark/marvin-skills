## Evaluation Criteria

1. **Comprehension**: Can someone without an economics degree understand this? Are technical terms explained when first used?
2. **Personal Relevance**: Does the document connect abstract policy mechanics to concrete personal impact? "Your CD earning 4% while inflation runs 5% means you're losing purchasing power"
3. **Actionability**: After reading, does the reader know what to DO? Or just what's happening to them?
4. **Urgency Calibration**: Does the document create appropriate urgency without being alarmist? Is the risk framed honestly?
5. **Accessibility Gaps**: Where does the writing assume knowledge the target reader doesn't have?
6. **Practical Barriers**: Do recommendations account for realistic constraints — $10K I-bond limits, minimum account sizes, tax complexity?
7. **Emotional Impact**: Does the reader feel informed and empowered, or overwhelmed and helpless?
8. **Trust Signals**: Are sources credible and varied? Is the analysis balanced or does it feel like gold-bug propaganda?

## Output Notes

- Flag every passage where jargon isn't explained (e.g., "SLR," "HQLA," "r-g differential")
- Identify the biggest "so what?" gaps — places where the analysis stops at description without connecting to personal impact
- Grade each document on a 1-5 accessibility scale
- Suggest specific rewrites to make the most impactful passages more concrete and personal
