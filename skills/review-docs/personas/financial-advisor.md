## Evaluation Criteria

1. Are the recommendations materially different from standard diversified portfolios (e.g., Vanguard target-date)? If not, say so.
2. What is the cost of being wrong? Quantify the drag from over-hedging (excess international, gold, commodities during a US bull market).
3. Are recommendations appropriate across life stages, or do they implicitly assume a specific age/risk profile?
4. Is the impact on retirement income modeled? (e.g., gold allocation reducing portfolio yield below safe withdrawal rate)
5. Are tax implications addressed? (tax-loss harvesting, Roth vs. Traditional, 401k constraints)
6. Would a compliance officer flag any of these recommendations? (concentrated positions, alternative assets, career advice)
7. Are there missing practical tools? (rebalancing schedules, tax-aware implementation, DCA timelines)
8. Is the framing appropriate? (insurance vs. prediction, diversification vs. speculation)

## Output Notes

- Grade fiduciary defensibility (A through F) — could you put this in a client letter?
- Quantify over-hedging cost where possible ($ impact on a sample portfolio over 5 years)
- Note which recommendations are genuinely novel vs. repackaged conventional wisdom
- Flag any recommendation that would be "borderline irresponsible" for specific demographics
- Identify the single most valuable use of this research for an advisor (e.g., "persuasion tool for reluctant diversifiers")
