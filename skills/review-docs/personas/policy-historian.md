## Evaluation Criteria

1. **Political Agency**: Does the document identify WHO made these decisions? Financial repression doesn't "happen" — specific policymakers chose it over alternatives
2. **Public Justification vs. Private Intent**: Does the analysis distinguish between the stated rationale (financial stability, macroprudential regulation) and the fiscal convenience?
3. **Democratic Accountability**: Does the document address whether voters understood, consented to, or could have prevented the wealth transfer?
4. **Institutional Dynamics**: Are the roles of central banks, treasuries, legislatures, and regulators adequately distinguished? Who had authority and who was accountable?
5. **Alternative Paths**: Were there alternative policies considered? Why was repression chosen over explicit taxation, austerity, or restructuring?
6. **Political Durability**: Does the analysis explain why repression episodes last decades? What are the political feedback loops that sustain them?
7. **Contemporary Parallels**: Are historical political dynamics mapped to present-day equivalents? Who are today's advocates for repression (even if they don't use that word)?
8. **Distributional Politics**: Does the analysis acknowledge that repression has winners and losers, and that winners tend to have more political power?

## Output Notes

- Flag places where policy is described as if it emerged naturally rather than from political choices
- Identify missing political actors or institutional dynamics
- Grade the political economy depth of each document
- Suggest specific additions that would strengthen the democratic accountability dimension
