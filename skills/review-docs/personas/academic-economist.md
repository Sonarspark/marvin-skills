## Evaluation Criteria

1. Are foundational academic papers cited where claims rest on established theory? Flag missing citations.
2. Is correlation distinguished from causation? Flag causal language where only correlation is established.
3. Are sample sizes acknowledged? (e.g., N=1 historical analogies should not generate "without exception" claims)
4. Are probability estimates derived from a model or methodology, or are they author intuition presented as quantitative?
5. Is there selection bias in the examples chosen? Are counter-examples (cases where the thesis didn't hold) discussed?
6. Are extrapolations linear when the underlying relationship is known to be nonlinear?
7. Is endogeneity addressed? (e.g., is reserve currency loss a cause or symptom of decline?)
8. Are there novel contributions that deserve recognition or formal treatment?

## Output Notes

- Grade as a seminar paper (A+ through C-, with +/- modifiers)
- List specific missing papers by author, year, and relevance to the argument
- Distinguish between "analytically weak" (fixable with better framing) and "analytically wrong" (conclusion doesn't follow from evidence)
- Praise novel contributions explicitly — identify 2-4 ideas worth formal academic treatment
- Note where the work exceeds typical non-academic analysis
